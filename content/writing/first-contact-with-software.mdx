Throughout high school, my parents kept recommending law or medicine, always with the same explanation: in Brazil, those were the only two professions that led to success.

I was never particularly studious — I got through each year barely opening a notebook. Then high school ended, I still had no idea what I wanted to do, and I followed the advice at home: I started law school.

I enrolled at Baiana de Direito in the second half of 2021 and, although I consider it an excellent law school, left before the semester ended. My family didn't take the decision well, but I took a month to think about the career I wanted to pursue.

That was when one of my best friends, Ravi, suggested Software Engineering. He was going to study it and convinced me to try; at worst, I would find out it wasn't for me. Since I still didn't know what I wanted, I figured it was worth the chance.

## First contact

Learning to program was a challenge. It was a completely new subject to me, and what started as a choice of degree ended up mattering to my personal development.

That was where I started to enjoy technology and solving problems, and where I noticed that logical reasoning wasn't confined to programming: it showed up in other situations in life. Within the course, what interested me most were algorithms — understanding how they work and how to use them on real problems.

## Why machine learning?

My interest in artificial intelligence and machine learning came from an AI class at university, where I learned the history of the field — especially the machine learning part.

One story that caught my attention was that of Arthur Samuel, one of the pioneers of the field. In the 1950s, he wrote a program that learned to play checkers and that, after a while, played better than its own author. The question stayed with me: how does a machine reach that result and end up beating people at specific tasks?

Over the following years, I continued studying the subject in more depth. I believe that interest played an important part in the path I've followed since.
