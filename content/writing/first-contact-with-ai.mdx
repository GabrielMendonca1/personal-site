When I started using AI during ChatGPT's beta, what surprised me was seeing a computer answer my questions and hold a conversation with me. That curiosity led me to study how the technology worked, what it was capable of, and what people hoped to achieve with it.

Along the way, I read articles and watched videos by Ilya Sutskever and Dario Amodei, and followed Sam Altman and Greg Brockman's discussions of the subject. That was how I came across scaling laws.

## An opportunity

Through that reading, I began to see AI as a change comparable to the rise of the internet or the creation of Bitcoin, perhaps with even greater effects. I didn't know how long that transformation would take, but I believed the technology would play a central role in society. I saw an opportunity to specialize.

## The impact on my profession

It was around then that I came to believe my profession would disappear. Not in some distant future: in the near term, and not only programming, but several other professions in the digital world. I reached that conclusion three or four years ago, before the discussion became as prominent as it is today.

Day to day, though, the most concrete effect was on productivity. I started building systems and websites, and that ability helped me get an internship and then a job, before I had graduated. All of this while many professors were dismissing the technology as limited, with no future.

In less than four years, AI became central to discussions about the future among companies and governments. For me, that change had already begun at work: in what I could produce and in the opportunities that came from it.
